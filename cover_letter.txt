==========================================================================
SUMMARY
==========================================================================

I show that a two-step Floquet update on the DDD substrate produces propagating spinor excitations with linear dispersion (Weyl points) and a kinematic clock-rate that obeys the Lorentz time-dilation factor 1/sqrt(1 - v^2/c^2) operationally to numerical precision, as measured by a Galilean-shifted k-projection test. The result complements the gravitational time dilation of Paper II by adding the kinematic component, completing the GR-like time structure on a discrete substrate.

==========================================================================
WHY THIS MATTERS
==========================================================================

Lorentz invariance is one of the most stringently tested symmetries of physics; reproducing it operationally on a fixed lattice (which a priori breaks rotational and boost invariance) is a non-trivial test. The Weyl-point structure is also the precondition for the gauge sector developed in Paper VI.

==========================================================================
NOVELTY
==========================================================================

Two-step Floquet rule on DDD. Numerical demonstration of Weyl points and linear dispersion. Operational test of the Lorentz factor via Galilean-shifted k-projection. Comparison with continuum Weyl semimetals.
